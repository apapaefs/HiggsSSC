# GEM's electromagnetic isolation and identification

The reference is the 1993 lepton-tagged diphoton analysis at 40 TeV. Its genuine photon, electron supplying the fake photon, and separate tagging electron all have transverse momentum above 20 GeV and absolute pseudorapidity below 2.5. The reconstructed diphoton pair must have transverse momentum above 40 GeV. The 5 GeV isolation allowance applies to all three objects. The cone radius is 0.45 for each photon candidate and 0.30 for the tagged electron. GEM Table 19 supplies the final electron-fake densities, while its intermediate yields describe the Higgs signal. [GEM TN-93-373, §5.1 and Table 19](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/other/ssc/ssc-gem-tn-93-373.pdf#page=60)

## What the calorimeter measured

GEM summed the transverse energies of towers with centers inside the isolation cone, subtracted the energy assigned to the candidate's 5 × 5 electromagnetic cluster and allowed a 5 GeV excess above the mean thermal and pileup energy. The cluster subtraction concerns energy in electromagnetic cells. Energy deposited in the underlying hadron calorimeter contributes to the cone. The nominal central electromagnetic cells spanned approximately 0.026 × 0.026 in pseudorapidity and azimuth, and the hadronic towers approximately 0.08 × 0.08. The fast simulation used progressively coarser segmentation in the endcap. The electromagnetic cell widths in Figure 3 are approximately 0.026 for absolute pseudorapidity below 1.56, 0.037 from 1.56 to 1.90, 0.052 from 1.90 to 2.20 and 0.075 from 2.20 to 2.66. These numbers are read from the grid drawn by the authors. [GEM Technical Design Report, §§2.2.3 and 2.3.1.2](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/other/ssc/ssc-gem-tn-93-262.pdf#page=46) · [Isolation equation and cluster definition](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/other/ssc/ssc-gem-tn-93-373.pdf#page=17) · [GEMFAST segmentation, Fig. 3](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/other/ssc/ssc-gem-tn-93-376.pdf#page=9)

Cleland and Vanyashin give the noise-only probability that an otherwise empty cone fails isolation. Their study corresponds to the SSC design luminosity of 10³³ cm⁻² s⁻¹. They evaluated a cone of radius 0.45 at pseudorapidity 0.117. For cells with absolute transverse energy above 0.5 GeV, the mean cone noise was 1.48 GeV. The failure probabilities for allowances above that mean were as follows. [GEM TN-93-376, Table 1](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/other/ssc/ssc-gem-tn-93-376.pdf#page=6)

| Allowance above the mean | Efficiency loss |
| ---: | ---: |
| 3 GeV | 11.0% |
| 4 GeV | 7.6% |
| 5 GeV | 5.4 ± 0.4% |

The positive tail determines the loss of well-isolated objects. A Gaussian with the fitted core width of 1.6–1.7 GeV has a probability of only 0.09–0.16% above a 5 GeV excess. The source provides a histogram of the thresholded distribution as well as the table. The scan spans a finite energy range, so digitization of the visible points alone need not reproduce the upper-tail integrals or the quoted mean. These are separate checks on any reconstruction. [GEM TN-93-373, Fig. 6](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/other/ssc/ssc-gem-tn-93-373.pdf#page=24) · [Cleland and Vanyashin, Fig. 11](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/other/ssc/ssc-gem-tn-93-376.pdf#page=17)

The pileup study fitted the Gaussian core width of the EM-plus-hadronic **pileup-only** sum as proportional to cone area to the power 0.94. Applying that dependence to radii 0.30 and 0.45 gives a width ratio of 0.47. Independent uniform per-cell noise would scale as the square root of area and give 0.67. The measured thresholded thermal-plus-pileup tail is available for the larger cone. Using either width relation to rescale its entire distribution to the tagging-electron cone introduces an assumption. Applying independent draws to the three cones also approximates the original noise overlays, which maintained spatial and longitudinal correlations among cells. [Cleland and Vanyashin, §§2–4](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/other/ssc/ssc-gem-tn-93-376.pdf#page=4)

## Energy from the candidate's own shower

GEM's 1993 barrel photon study gives an approximate 95% containment for the sum over 5 × 5 electromagnetic towers. The remaining energy includes energy deposited upstream of the calorimeter, energy outside the sides of the cluster and energy reaching the hadronic calorimeter. Only the portion that enters the measured isolation cone and contributes to the thresholded energy sum can change the isolation decision. A shower can also raise a cell containing other energy above threshold. The 95% figure does not give this portion or an event-by-event bound. The paper tabulates energies from 25 to 320 GeV for its energy reconstruction study. [Klimenko, GEM TN-93-463, introduction and Table 1](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/other/ssc/ssc-gem-tn-93-463.pdf#page=2)

An earlier GEM barrel simulation reports separate mean leakage energies. For incident energies of 20, 50 and 80 GeV, the mean energy leaking out the sides of a **7 × 7** tower array was 0.19, 0.46 and 0.71 GeV. Mean backward leakage was 0.085, 0.27 and 0.54 GeV. The missing ring between 5 × 5 and 7 × 7, the cell threshold, cone acceptance and differences in calorimeter geometry prevent these numbers from fixing the contamination under the tagged selection. [GEM TN-92-115, slide 69](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/other/ssc/ssc-gem-tn-92-115.pdf#page=61)

The 1992 tagged analysis provides an event-level cross-check of the cone dependence. With pair transverse momentum above 40 GeV and the tag cone held at 0.30, the expected electron-fake background went from 34 to 32 to 30 events as both photon cones increased from 0.30 to 0.45 to 0.60. The corresponding genuine diphoton yields for a 120 GeV Higgs were 22, 18 and 15. This version used a 3 × 3 cluster, a transverse-energy allowance of 5 GeV plus 10% of the candidate transverse energy, and idealized calorimeter coverage. The final 1993 four-lepton study separately reported a 7% relative loss of signal events when the lepton cones increased from 0.30 to 0.35. These tests measure how many events pass when the cone radius changes. They include energy from the rest of each event. Neither study supplies the absolute single-object isolation efficiency of a genuine 20–100 GeV shower for the final tagged selection. The effective fraction of candidate transverse energy entering the thresholded isolation sum therefore remains a parameter to vary. [1992 isolation definition](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/other/ssc/ssc-gem-tn-92-126.pdf#page=20) · [1992 tagged selection and Table 15](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/other/ssc/ssc-gem-tn-92-126.pdf#page=33) · [1993 four-lepton comparison, §6.2](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/other/ssc/ssc-gem-tn-93-373.pdf#page=66)

## What the shower-shape number measures

GEMFAST supplied isolation and acceptance. The detailed GEANT identification used three 11 × 11 barrel patches to simulate showers near the isolated candidate. The algorithm used first-layer strip energies, lateral moments, and pointing from deeper layers, together with hadronic leakage and a track/cluster momentum match for a tagged electron. GEM quotes a working efficiency of about 90% for isolated single photons or electrons. Its often-quoted factor of four from shower shape applies to the remaining **jet** background. For the single electromagnetic candidates, the simulation already includes three 90% factors, one each for the genuine photon, the fake electron's photon identification, and the tagging electron's identification. An actual efficiency change must replace the corresponding factor. The 80–85% overall photon identification efficiency quoted later in the design report includes geometrical losses. [GEM TN-93-373, §§2.1–2.2](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/other/ssc/ssc-gem-tn-93-373.pdf#page=18) · [GEM Technical Design Report, pp. 2-13–2-14](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/other/ssc/ssc-gem-tn-93-262.pdf#page=53) · [Current object probabilities](../../response.md)

Ma and Leltchouk's calorimeter study shows how the shower-shape working point was chosen. They calibrated the expected shower width and pointing with single photons, correcting for the impact position within the tower. They fixed the single-photon acceptance at 90% and determined the rejection of neutral pions versus energy. The discriminant plots at 25 GeV and the strip-noise test characterize single-photon versus multi-photon identification. They provide no measurement of the shower shape of an electron classified as a photon by the tracker. [GEM TN-92-257, pp. 9–14](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/other/ssc/ssc-gem-tn-92-257.pdf#page=10)

GEM's separate tracker study used isolated electromagnetic clusters from Drell–Yan electron pairs and Higgs photon pairs. It counted hits along the line from the reconstructed vertex to the cluster, including the fraction of tracker planes without a hit. Section 5.1 quotes a 3% electron misidentification rate at 96% photon acceptance for the tagged analysis. The table of alternative tracking working points gives the tracking efficiency averaged over the simulated control samples. The study gives no value of the shower-shape efficiency **conditional on an electron having failed tracking and become a photon candidate in eeγ**. The marginal 90% single-shower and 3% tracker probabilities alone place no nontrivial bound on that conditional efficiency. The rare 3% could fall entirely within the failing or passing showers while maintaining both marginals. [GEM tracker study and Table 15](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/other/ssc/ssc-gem-tn-93-373.pdf#page=45) · [Tagged-channel working point](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/other/ssc/ssc-gem-tn-93-373.pdf#page=61)

For the sole purpose of measuring the suppression required, let the re-evaluated tagged cross section with the default 90% fake-candidate calorimeter efficiency be σ. Holding the other selections and tracker probability fixed, equality with GEM's 0.0374 fb requires a conditional fake-electron calorimeter efficiency of 0.90 × (0.0374 fb / σ). This inversion is a diagnostic. The original conditional efficiency would require paired shower and tracker observables, or intermediate yields from GEM's eeγ sample. Stable-particle overlap tests can probe energy sharing in crowded clusters. The first-layer strip energies, fluctuations of shower profiles within material and the tracker-hit road remain independent missing inputs. [Shower-shape likelihood inputs and working point](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/other/ssc/ssc-gem-tn-93-373.pdf#page=18)

The 1993 software guide locates GEMFAST in the SSC's local CVS software tree. The public technical-note archive documents that framework and a GFLASH–SIGEM prototype. The sources examined here provide neither the tagged eeγ event record nor a runnable version of its detector simulation and identification with the original response inputs.
