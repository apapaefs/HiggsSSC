# From a showered collision to the tagged continuum

The detector response assigns a probability to each diphoton candidate in a showered collision. It requires two reconstructed photons above 20 GeV within |η| < 2.5, a distinct electron or muon above 20 GeV in the tracker acceptance, and reconstructed diphoton transverse momentum above 40 GeV. Electromagnetic objects use the SSC calorimeter smearing and fiducial cracks. Muons use their shower momenta. In a cone of radius 0.45 around either genuine photon, the sum of other stable visible transverse momenta must be below 5 GeV. The cone radius for the lepton is 0.30. The scalar sum excludes muons and neutrinos. This approximates calorimeter isolation in a single collision. The smearing includes the project’s pileup-noise term. [GEM tagged selection and historical tracker working point](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/other/ssc/ssc-gem-tn-93-373.pdf#page=60)

After geometry and isolation, a genuine photon passes with probability 0.90 × 0.96. An isolated electron passes photon identification with probability 0.90/33. GEM gives the conditional tracker rejection of 33 for this analysis. The tagged background uses this working point throughout 80–140 GeV. The existing tagged signal measurement uses the same photon efficiency at 125 GeV and tighter photon track matching for reconstructed masses within 10 GeV of the Z mass. Electron and muon tag probabilities are 0.90 and 0.85 × 0.95. The event trigger probability is 0.988. [Historical electron and genuine photon identification](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/other/ssc/ssc-gem-tn-93-373.pdf#page=61) · [SSC detector response](../../../../../hgammagamma/SSC_DETECTOR_RESPONSE.md)

A jet promoted to a photon uses the electromagnetic energy response and the inclusive GEM jet-to-isolated-photon transfer. The transfer already includes jet isolation. Jets within ΔR < 0.45 of an isolated photon or possible tagged lepton cannot supply another photon. A pair containing a jet must also satisfy ΔR ≥ 0.45. All jets use the interpolated 125 GeV quark transfer as the nominal assignment. Replacing the transfer for jets without a b tag by the gluon value supplies a second spectrum. This working point is held fixed when scanning the diphoton mass. The rates are calibrated detector assumptions, so the simulated mass spectrum checks the collision backgrounds conditional on those assumptions. [Transfer probabilities in the SSC response](../../../../../hgammagamma/LOAnalysis/Code/HwSimPostAnalysis_gammagamma_SSC.cc)

Photon candidates compete in descending reconstructed transverse momentum. For every possible leading pair, the response multiplies its two identification probabilities by the failure probabilities of higher-ranked candidates outside the pair. It then requires at least one distinct identified lepton. Classifying an electron as a photon and identifying it as a lepton are mutually exclusive outcomes. If an electron above the pair failed photon identification, its conditional tag efficiency is the unconditional efficiency divided by its photon-failure probability. This includes both possible assignments in e⁺e⁻γ events and all eligible jet pairs in a top decay. The response packet lists the mass, nominal probability and alternative jet-transfer probability for each outcome of each collision. An entry with zero probability records a collision that failed selection.

## Hard photons in top decays

The baseline top samples tt̄, tt̄γ and tt̄γγ require respectively zero, one or two selected photons connected by photon-copy ancestry to a hard production photon. Additional genuine photons in those samples must originate in top or W decay, including radiation from the decay products. Traversal stops at hadrons, whose photons enter through the jet transfer. The dedicated matrix-element calculation uses one radiative top decay with zero or one production photon, two photons in one top decay, or one photon from each top. Its spectrum replaces the selected shower-decay photon outcomes in the baseline tt̄ and tt̄γ samples. The other baseline outcomes continue to contribute.

For the four dedicated samples, the saved shower’s two incoming parton IDs, beam directions and energies identify the collision in its source LHE block. Its LHE mother indices classify each photon as production or decay. The private HwSim plugin records the four-momentum of every photon copy in the ancestry tree. A surviving shower photon must have an ancestor on its photon-copy chain whose four-vector agrees with the input LHE photon to 10⁻⁵ of its energy in every component. Each LHE photon can match at most one selected photon. This allows the stable photon direction to change under recoil. In a fixed, 250-event Zγ shower the method matched the original hard photon in 244 events, with agreement on the identity of every stable photon against HwSim’s independent outgoing-hard-particle marker. Simple angular matching of the stable photon to the LHE direction within ΔR < 0.15 found 131.

The optional dileptonic enrichment isolates the electron-fake contribution in the one-decay-photon top sample. Both top decays must produce an electron or muon directly or through a W with the appropriate charge. The truth classifier follows copies of the lepton and W. It excludes leptons originating in tau, hadron and photon decays. The inclusive one-decay sample supplies all jet-fake outcomes and the electron-fake outcomes outside the dileptonic class. The separate dileptonic matrix element supplies electron-fake outcomes in that class, normalized to its own LHE cross section. This calculation requires the classification on every contributing one-decay shard. Use `--top-dileptonic` after validating the closure of the dedicated sample.

## Absolute normalization and MC covariance

Let $w_i$ be the shower weight and $p_{ib}$ the sum of all detector outcomes from collision $i$ that enter mass bin $b$. Within a hard integration, the selected rate in that bin is $\sigma_{\mathrm{hard}}\sum_i w_i p_{ib}/\sum_i w_i$. The denominator includes every attempted hard collision. A failed shower contributes zero to the numerator. Sibling shower shards share the integration cross section and its uncertainty. Independent integrations of one process enter proportionally to their generated collision counts. Distinct hard processes add. The source document records LHE hashes, event intervals, shower seeds, packet hashes and integration IDs, and the reader rejects duplicate seeds, event blocks and overlapping hard-event ranges.

The finite-sample covariance uses each *collision* as one independent draw. It includes correlations across candidate pairs, across bins and with the signed weight denominator. A hard-integration error contributes the outer product of its accepted bin fractions times its cross-section variance. The 123.3 and 126.7 GeV boundaries are exact edges. For a positive unit-weight stratum with zero selected collisions, the output also reports a one-sided binomial upper limit conditional on its hard cross section. The limit uses all attempted collisions, an event acceptance bounded by the trigger and, where the two specific genuine photons must pass, their identification. The simultaneous 95% limits allow for examining both the Higgs window and the full simulated range across the required hard samples. A Poisson or detector uncertainty in a real SSC dataset is separate from the quoted finite-MC error.

The optional sideband check excludes the entire 123.3–126.7 GeV interval from each fit. It fits an exponential of a first-, second- or third-degree polynomial, integrated over each sideband, using the full simulated covariance. The two fit ranges are 100–145 and 110–145 GeV. The per-model output gives fit probabilities, effective counts and closure against the withheld peak, including correlated source events. The reported range across models quantifies dependence on the chosen interval and polynomial degree. Sparse radiative or fake sources require their own checks even when the summed continuum fits well.
