# Study questions: a Higgs discovery at the SSC

This is a counterfactual research outline, not a prediction of an observed
experiment. Assume a completed 40 TeV pp collider, a 125 GeV Standard Model
Higgs and a GEM-like detector. Historical detector forecasts and their sources
are collected in [SSCInfo.md](SSCInfo.md); neither GEM nor SDC was built or
calibrated at the SSC.

## Questions the simulation can address

1. How much luminosity would a narrow diphoton excess need under the assumed
   energy resolution and fake rates?
2. How would `H -> ZZ* -> 4l` support that excess, and how sensitive is it to
   lepton acceptance, isolation and reducible backgrounds?
3. Which conclusions change when historical rates and counting estimates are
   replaced by modern theory and likelihood-based statistics?

Compare a historically motivated cut baseline, a modern-theory update and a
modern-statistics update separately. Stress-test photon resolution, pointing,
isolation, jet/electron fakes, lepton efficiency, pileup and background
systematics. Reproducing the qualitative LHC discovery behaviour at 7–8 TeV
would be a useful external cross-check of the simplified method.

Useful deliverables are significance versus luminosity, luminosity versus
mass resolution or fake rate, channel comparisons and historical-versus-modern
assumption variations. A counting `S/sqrt(B)` or exploratory classifier score
alone cannot establish a discovery reach.

## Historical branches

An early-to-mid-2000s SSC discovery is a scenario to explore, contingent on
construction delays, commissioning, luminosity and detector readiness. A 1999
discovery should not be assumed. The Tevatron, LEP and LHC retain independent
scientific roles.

LEP's roughly 209 GeV energy was below the on-shell threshold
`mZ + mH ≈ 216.2 GeV` for the actual Higgs mass. A short extension at the same
energy differs fundamentally from an upgrade to 220–230 GeV or a high-luminosity
240 GeV Higgs factory. Those upgrades are separate counterfactual investments,
not consequences implied by SSC survival.

A surviving SSC might have shifted the timing and location of discovery and
high-mass searches. It does not follow that it would surpass the LHC in every
coupling measurement: modern tracking, triggers, reconstruction, statistics and
integrated luminosity also matter. If no accessible new states accompanied the
Higgs, an earlier discovery could also have brought forward the tension between
a light Higgs and null searches. Present these as conditional interpretations,
not outputs already established by this repository.
